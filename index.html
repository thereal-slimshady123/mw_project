1. Motivation & Context

Intense mental work quickly burns through our attention spans and slows our reaction times. Most people deal with this by taking short screen breaks or resting quietly, but these resets often fail to actually restore cognitive clarity.

While guided breathing and mindfulness apps attempt to solve this, their repetitive synthetic voices and robotic counts can easily become another cognitive distraction.

BREATHEBEAT explores a more intuitive alternative: mapping structured breathing patterns onto melodic audio synthesized in real time. Rooted in Indian classical music, the system translates paced diaphragmatic breathing directly into the organic glides of Raga Bhupali.

2. Why Raga Bhupali Works

Clean Pentatonic Scale (Audava):

By using only five notes (Sa, Re, Ga, Pa, Dha) and skipping tension-bearing tones like Ma and Ni, the raga avoids unresolved dissonance. Every note harmonizes smoothly with the drone.

Mirrored Contours:

The ascent (Arohana) and descent (Avarohana) mirror each other identically, creating a smooth arc that naturally mimics the expansion and contraction of the chest.

Natural Resting Note (Vadi):

The raga's primary emphasis note (Vadi) is Ga, which matches the pitch held during the breath retention (Kumbhaka) phase, providing a stable auditory center.

Naturally Calming Character:

Traditionally associated with calm, reflective, and meditative settings, Bhupali avoids sharp or agitated melodic turns that could raise cognitive arousal.

3. Direct Audio-to-Breath Mapping (4-2-6 Ratio)

Breath Phase Duration Classical Svara Movement Acoustic Physiology
Inhale (Puraka) 4 seconds Arohana: Sa → Re → Ga → Pa → Dha → Sa′ Ascending pitch sweep with swelling volume to signal air intake naturally.
Hold (Kumbhaka) 2 seconds Vadi Note: Ga (E4) sustained Pitch pauses and sustains steadily on dominant root over drone; anchors retention without tension.
Exhale (Rechaka) 6 seconds Avarohana: Sa′ → Dha → Pa → Ga → Re → Sa Gentle descending glide tapering in volume to prompt complete, relaxed release and vagal nerve tone.

4. Metrics & Evaluation Methodology

To keep our findings clear and actionable, the study narrows evaluation to three core indicators:

  • 1. Cognitive Recovery Score: Recovery = (X_fatigue - X_recovery) / (X_fatigue - X_baseline) × 100% Derived from pre- and post-break Stroop reaction times (RT in ms) and accuracy (%). A score near 100% reflects complete return to baseline capability.
  • 2. Perceived Effort: Measures how mentally demanding or distracting the breathing condition felt, captured via a quick post-session NASA-TLX ease/effort survey.
  • 3. Musical Familiarity: Brief intake rating (1–5) of prior exposure to Indian classical music to verify whether calming impact is universal.
